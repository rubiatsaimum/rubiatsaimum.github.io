Research agenda

Research

My research connects regional security, South Asian politics, maritime affairs, migration, and political discourse.

Security communities

Anchor states, regional order, and stable peace.

My doctoral research examines how major regional powers, or “anchor states,” shape the formation, maintenance, and potential erosion of security communities. I focus on how domestic political processes, strategic interests, and foreign-policy choices interact across North America, Southeast Asia, and South Asia.

South Asian security

Crisis politics, strategic competition, and state choices.

I study South Asian security with particular attention to Bangladesh, India, Pakistan, and the Bay of Bengal. Current interests include India–Pakistan public diplomacy, defence diversification, regional hierarchy, and the strategic choices of smaller states.

Maritime & Indo-Pacific affairs

Governance, naval diplomacy, and strategic practice.

My maritime research covers freedom of navigation, maritime governance, naval modernization, defence relations, and the political consequences of an increasingly maritime Indo-Pacific.

Migration & nativism

Media discourse, internal migration, and refugee securitization.

My work on migration examines how political actors and media construct migrants and refugees as social, economic, and security problems. This includes comparative research on nativist discourse and research on the securitization of Rohingya refugees.
