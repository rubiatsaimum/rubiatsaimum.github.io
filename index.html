About

International security, diplomacy, and migration.

I am a PhD student in the Department of Political Studies at Queen’s University and a graduate researcher at the Centre for International and Defence Policy (CIDP). My doctoral research examines how major regional powers, or “anchor states,” enable or constrain security communities in their regions, and under what conditions they contribute to their destabilization.

My broader research spans South Asian security, maritime security and governance, middle-power diplomacy, strategic narratives, and migration. Methodologically, I use a mixed-methods approach that combines process tracing and network analysis with text-as-data methods and content analysis.

Before joining Queen’s, I completed an MA in Political Science at Memorial University of Newfoundland. I previously taught maritime security and strategic studies at Bangladesh Maritime University and worked as a Research Officer at the Bangladesh Institute of Maritime Research and Development (BIMRAD).

Research

Four overlapping areas structure my current work.

Regional security & order

Security communities, anchor states, regional hierarchy, strategic autonomy, and foreign-policy practice.

South Asian security

India–Pakistan crises, Bangladesh’s strategic choices, public diplomacy, and regional geopolitics.

Maritime & Indo-Pacific affairs

Maritime governance, freedom of navigation, naval diplomacy, defence relations, and Indo-Pacific strategy.

Migration & nativism

Internal migration, refugee securitization, nativist discourse, media framing, and political identity.
